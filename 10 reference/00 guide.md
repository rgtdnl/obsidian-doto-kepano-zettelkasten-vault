# 10 Reference: what caught your attention

**Purpose:** reference notes, one long note per source (book, podcast, video, article, conversation, even your own journal), holding brief citations to what caught your attention. They are the reservoir main notes are made from, and the record of everything you've engaged with.

The book: a reference note is a single long-note containing brief citations or "references" to what caught your attention while reading... just enough information to bring the idea back to the front of your mind (Luhmann's method).

## What lives here
- **Reference notes:** one per source, with locators (page numbers, timestamps, track numbers) and a few words per capture.

## Rules
- One source, one note. The filename is the source.
- Captures are brief: locator + a few words. No long summaries. Don't hoard whole quotes; the text piles up and the note becomes unwieldy.
- Cite anything that catches your attention, even if you don't know why yet. Reading is not note-taking.
- Not every capture becomes a main note. The reference note stays; it is the record.
- Status field tracks [[to read]] / [[reading]] / [[read]].
- Owned books: mark the margins, pull what still interests you into the note later. Borrowed books: take the note separately, you keep the notes after the book goes back.

## Action
1. While reading: jot locators + phrases (marginalia if you own the book; a separate note if borrowed).
2. When finished, file the reference note here and set status to [[read]].
3. Later: turn what still interests you into main notes (template: `main.md`). Recurring themes in your captures are clues to what you actually care about.
